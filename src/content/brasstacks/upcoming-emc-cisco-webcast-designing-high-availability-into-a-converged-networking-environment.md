<p>Please join J Metz of Cisco and I (Erik Smith of EMC) for a&#0160;webcast that will&#0160;cover some of&#0160;the HA&#0160;considerations that&#0160;you&#0160;need to think about before moving to a Converged Nework topology.&#0160;</p>
<p>The webcast details are provided below.&#0160; Please remember to pre-register.</p>
<p><strong>Date:</strong>&#0160; November 10, 2011</p>
<p><strong>Time:</strong>&#0160; 12-1PM Eastern</p>
<p><strong>URL:</strong>&#0160; <a href="http://cemc.bright-talk.com/">http://cemc.bright-talk.com/</a></p>
<p><strong>Title:</strong>&#0160; Designing High Availability in a Converged Networking Environment&#0160;</p>
<p><strong>Abstract:</strong>&#0160;&#0160; Highly available networks have different meanings for LAN and SAN administrators. This session will explore the design principles for a truly converged system, and identify the means by which architects can build highly available networks for both LANs and SANs using converged technologies like FCoE and DCB-based Ethernet.</p>
<p>Particular importance will be paid to Role-Based Access and SAN best practices using a converged infrastructure, along with recommendations for growth and scale.</p>
<p>Join us for this event and learn more about:</p>
<ul>
<li>the requirements for High Availability for both LANs and SANs</li>
<li>the limitations of designing highly available networks and the approaches to mitigate issues within the Data Center</li>
<li>how Highly Available converged networks scale with increased storage requirements over time</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Who Should Attend:</strong>&#0160; Storage and Networking Administrators who want to learn more designing high availability converged networks.</p>
<p><strong>NOTE:&#0160; Please&#0160;remember that you&#0160;need to register in advance.</strong></p>
<p>Looking forward to seeing you there!</p>
<p>Thanks for reading!</p>
